# Verification status

The project has a kernel-checked foundation library, a straight-line byte resource checker, and a pure functional fragment with executable elaboration, and a structured program model with typed heap reads and writes. The sequential formal gate remains open.

| Reference Rust call sites | Binds each imported crate and symbol to its source argument order, field paths, and access modes. ## Reference source checker

The byte-record fragment supports one flat record with distinct `u8` fields, initialization in declaration order, literal and computed stores, discarded reads, nested lexical views, and a final field read. Computed stores include copying bytes from a field or view and unsigned saturating subtraction by a byte literal. Control flow includes byte comparisons in `if` and `else`, finite ranges with byte endpoints, and measured `while` countdowns. A helper takes one record parameter with a `~>.{...}` field contract, calls earlier declarations, and returns unit, a byte, or a shared or exclusive view of a contracted field. The reference grammar uses trailing commas in record field lists and semicolons after statements and block expressions.

Branch guards can compare two current byte places. The verifier checks authority for both reads, and the interpreter observes the left byte before the right. Branch arms preserve entry resource metadata, so the verifier checks the common continuation once. A finite range snapshots its two byte endpoints from left to right. `Operations.range_matches_for` connects the compact run to an independent half-open range traversal, including the exact endpoint reads. The resource checker checks the body once against the entry invariant. It can skip the body for empty literal ranges, ranges with equal resolved places, ranges starting at `255u8`, and ranges ending at `0u8`. Both endpoints still require read authority and the body must resolve. Helper bodies are checked over arbitrary initialized byte contents for the record size. Inlined calls are also checked under the caller's actual live reservations. A formal frame theorem preserves each field outside the helper's declared contract under the record handle invariant.

`Comparison.constantResult_sound` proves type-bound guards such as `byte <= 255u8`. A comparison can also be decided when both operands resolve to the same place. The resource checker can skip an infeasible arm while checking authority for every guard read. Both arms still undergo name and contract resolution and appear in reference MIR. Return laws reuse the type-bound proof over larger finite domains. The three-byte fixture proves a law over 16,777,216 inputs without enumeration. A `when` clause filters the initial values of its resolved input field, and the search budget counts the resulting product of per-field domains. In a law such as `always limit(row) <= row.left`, the right operand comes from the initial record even when the helper writes to that field.

A byte-returning call reads its result after the callee body and before the caller writes the destination. `FunctionMIR.result_observed` identifies the corresponding byte-machine read. `SourceTrace.returned_view_origin` links each view result to the declared parameter and field. `FunctionMIR.view_result_reserved` proves that testing an isolated view result opens a legal reservation of the field's handle and releases it before teardown. A caller binds the returned reservation and can release it after its last use.

Explicit and returned views share the last-use analysis in `Body.loanWindow`. The split preserves the statement tokens and proves the view name is absent from the suffix. A branch, loop, or reborrow is kept together as a statement, with both arms, guards, bounds, and nested uses included in the scan. The suffix resolves remaining view slots after release. Byte `case` accepts one literal arm and a wildcard, evaluates the scrutinee once, and uses the same checked join as `if`.

A scalar reborrow such as `view shared child = parent { ... }` reuses a live parent reservation. `ReadAt.reborrow_scope` proves that the child resolves to the same slot and the parent name is unavailable inside that scope. The parent resumes at the continuation. The checker verifies the physical loan and rejects authority upgrades, including attempts through a nested shared child of an exclusive loan.

An exclusive view accepts literal stores, copies, saturating subtraction, or assignment from a byte-returning helper. The checker uses the same place representation and live reservation address check for loads and stores. `Place.view_read_origin` and `Place.view_write_origin` connect the claimed offset to the exact byte accessed and require exclusive authority for the store. A write counts as a use for loan liveness and as a mutation when checking whether a loop body preserves its measure.

The reference grammar permits explicit `move` steps directly after record initialization. `SourceTrace.current_owner_not_retired` proves the final binding is absent from the retired names. The checked MIR records the move chain and retains the same record allocation. Later field accesses and helper arguments resolve against the current binding.

`SourceMIR.byteLayout_conforms` and `byteLayout_minimal` establish conformance and minimum size for the reference record with independently addressable bytes and one-byte ABI slots. `Verified.initialized_layout_field` connects logical initialization to the allocated byte and its handle. General nested, packed, and collection layouts have separate models and proofs.

`Verified.rustField_initialized` connects a Rust slice descriptor to the actual initialized record and its declared field. `Verified.rustBorrowPlan_after_body` reduces preflight for those fields to a pairwise mode check. `Verified.rustTrace_after_body` proves that a finite authorized trace can complete after an accepted source body, returns its resource metadata, and frames every field outside the writable arguments. The trace checker validates each loan ID with two endpoint comparisons before expanding byte reservations.

The reference Rust fragment declares unit-returning imports with `@external(rust, "crate", "symbol")`. Each `[u8]` or `[mut u8]` parameter names a one-byte slice at the call site, such as `update(row.left, row.right)`. A moved owner must be referenced by its current name. `RustArgumentByte.Resolves.loan` connects an adapter access to the exact byte and reservation created from its argument. `MatchingRustMIR.corresponds` checks the imported symbols, argument modes, and ordered operations in supplied reference MIR. `VerifiedRustSource.run_total` proves execution for supplied authorized models, and `RustSourceExecutes.prefix_safe` covers allocation, local statements, each foreign reservation and access, and the final read and destruction.

Pure source programs support typed unary lambda parameters, local `let` bindings, higher-order calls, binary tuples, projection, and Boolean branches. Named helpers may take zero or multiple arguments, call earlier declarations, and declare function-valued outputs. The reference core packs an argument group into a right-associated product, with the same type as supplying the corresponding tuple to a unary function. `PureModuleTrace.typed` checks every named helper at its declared signature, including unused declarations. `PureLowers.unique` fixes which lexical binding any capture refers to. `CoreAST.infer_correct` relates executable checking to the declarative type and effect, including integer literal bounds. `CoreSemantics.reduce_correct` relates the executable reducer to the independent call-by-value step relation. `VerifiedPure.terminates` proves normalization and `VerifiedPure.run_total` proves the checker returns the resulting byte. `MatchingPureMIR.corresponds` binds supplied pure MIR to the same typed source operations and closure captures.

`Result(A, B)` supports explicit constructor annotations such as `Ok(42u8): Result(u8, bool)`. A match requires one `Ok` and one `Err` arm in either order. The checker gives each payload its variant's type and checks both arm results. The evaluator evaluates the scrutinee once and binds its payload only in the selected arm. `Normalizes.matchSum` covers nested and function-valued payloads. `proof_erasure_simulation` preserves the runtime tag and strips the payload type annotations.

A pure law such as `law both_variants(value: Result(u8, bool)) { always handles(value) }` resolves `handles` in the checked module with signature `fn(Result(u8, bool)) -> bool`. Using `never` requires a false result on each input. `VerifiedPureDefinition.terminates` derives normalization from the linked function's source lowering. `BoundPureLaw.proved_iff` derives the universal proposition from complete enumeration of typed values, and `BoundPureLaw.falsified_sound` links a counterexample to a reduction of the same function. The evaluator prepares the predicate closure once per law.

`CheckedWhile.run_correct` checks a well-founded loop against its actual guard reads and the operations executed in each iteration. The source grammar accepts a byte literal threshold in `while row.counter > 7u8 decreases row.counter` with a positive saturating decrement followed by checked statements that preserve the counter. `Operations.countdown_matches_while` proves that the compact MIR schedule runs the exact number of iterations and agrees with the independent while semantics, including every guard read and the final observation at or below the threshold. Calls and nested loops contribute their reachable writes. Statically infeasible arms and zero-iteration bodies contribute no writes to the measure check.

The scoped `fork`/`join` fragment admits a finite list of tasks containing discarded field reads and literal stores. Tasks can share reads, and a task can reread its own writes. `Operations.fork_agrees` proves that every interleaving respecting each task's access order preserves the parent resources and yields the same final bytes as the reference schedule. `TaskInterleave.read_at_cut` relates the byte at an interleaved read to the reader's own prefix run in isolation. The initial handle-to-field invariant connects the footprints to physical byte locations. Native parallel execution remains a separate correspondence obligation.

Collection programs with an explicit layout support literal byte-record rows, captured byte indices, nested views, conditional statements, and a final indexed read. `ByteCollection.entry_correct` checks the correspondence between initial row values and the allocated handles. `BranchFacts.byteCase_reachable` ensures the checker retains the actual index and associates equalities or inequalities with the captured values. A fresh load receives its own binding. `BranchFacts.byteCandidates_contains` proves that intersecting known half-open bounds retains the actual index, so the checker can skip indices outside the proved interval. `CollectionExecutes` specifies the complete source trace, and `VerifiedCollection.source_execution` connects it to checked execution. The separate `checkScopedIndexedBorrow_bracket` theorem uses symbolic bounds, field separation, and captured index inequalities to open and release borrow plans without enumerating index values. It assumes coverage of the caller's live views. The reference source collection checker currently verifies its feasible dispatch arms.

## Trust boundary

Proofs are checked by the installed Lean kernel. The allowed standard axiom set is `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`; the audit reports their actual use. No project-defined axioms or proof holes are permitted. The source scan is an additional conservative guard; it does not replace kernel checking or semantic review of theorem statements.

Executing the test runner additionally trusts Lean's compiler/runtime and the host toolchain (`rustc`/`cargo` and Zig 0.17.0). Foreign declarations (`@external(rust, ...)` functions and instances and legacy `extern "rust"` stubs) are **not** proved by Lean or verified against Rust semantics: `aethel verify` explicitly isolates them under `[FOREIGN] Foreign contracts assumed` per `Aethel.RustTraitBridge.verifyModuloForeignContract`, trusting the foreign implementation to uphold its declared C-ABI signature and memory contract. No theorem currently establishes correctness of a Zig implementation, Zig built-in backend lowering, a real allocator, general hardware concurrency, or source-to-machine compilation.

## Sequential formal gate status

The sequential formal gate remains open. The established guarantees apply to individual models and checker inputs.

- `CoreSoundness.progress` covers the `IsSequentialCore` expression fragment. `StatefulCore.stateful_progress` admits a blocked resource operation, which `stateful_progress_ready` rules out under `ResourceReady`. Reference verification now connects pure higher-order calls, initial record ownership transfers, and acyclic contracted calls to executable checking. General loops, recursive functions, effectful higher-order calls, arbitrary returned origins, and general layouts remain open.
- The byte-kernel, record, and collection models have allocation, update, or frame theorems under their respective hypotheses. The scalar-slot models use mathematical addresses and stores. Target-parametric allocation and compiler lowering require separate evidence.
- The MIR validator proves the stated properties of its submitted records, traces, and law propositions. For example, the combat fixture reports two Lean-checked symbolic law entries and one structural frame check. Production source-to-MIR and MIR-to-native correspondence and the full source-language litmus corpus remain gate requirements.

Use `law` for both forms. A declaration inside `type Character { … }`
applies at construction and after each update. A named equality law in a
namespace acquires the same semantics when the defining module adopts it
with `use law`. Standalone proposition checks quantify over their inputs.
